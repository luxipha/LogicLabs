- Prefers narration/voiceover audio as the master timing source: scene changes should land on narration idea boundaries, not arbitrary timestamps. Confidence: 0.9
- Prefers subtle, cinematic camera motion (Ken Burns zoom/pan with easing) and soft transitions (10–16 frame crossfades, subtle blur, gentle push); avoids spinning, flipping, flashy wipes, glitches, and aggressive camera movement. Treats hard cuts / jumpy scene changes between screens as a defect — every scene boundary must have an actual visible transition effect. Confidence: 0.95
- Prefers minimal on-screen text showing only key concepts, never word-for-word narration. Confidence: 0.85
- When the scene artwork already carries the narrative text (signs, speech bubbles, chalkboards, banners), prefers dropping the redundant overlaid heading entirely and letting the artwork speak — overlay text is only kept when it adds context the artwork doesn't have (e.g., removed "CAN YOU HELP US?" because the artwork had the speech bubble, and kept a short "IS A STORM COMING?" heading on a scene without such text). Confidence: 0.75
- Prefers narration clearly audible (≈1.0) over low background music (≈0.08–0.15) that ducks further during key lines; music should be clearly audible in the first and last ~6 seconds (bookends with short fades so the opening and closing read strongly), while the middle stays soft so narration dominates. Confidence: 0.85
- Treats a silent Studio preview as a defect: audio (narration, music, SFX) must be audible in the preview environment, not only in the final render. Confidence: 0.75
- Prefers a clean visual system: modern sans-serif, bold headings, high contrast, and a semantic palette (blue for engineering/tech, orange for action, red only for urgency). Confidence: 0.8
- Prefers 16:9 landscape layouts with large, readable text suitable for classroom projection; no vertical/mobile layouts. Confidence: 0.8
- Expects animated subjects to remain visible and centered in the frame for the entire animation — reports off-screen or out-of-view action as a defect (e.g., "the plane isn't in the middle of the canvas, so i can't see"), and likewise flags a loaded-but-invisible model in the WebGL canvas as a defect ("5 visible meshes ... but nothing is visible"; "nothing is showing visually there" on the radar lesson even though the console showed draw calls), so shots must be framed (explicit camera + motion constrained to the frustum) and 3D scenes must actually render the subject. Confidence: 0.9
- When a rendered video needs a changed voiceover, prefers rebuilding/re-timing the composition from source (reusing existing scene images/components, re-timed to the new narration) over editing the rendered MP4 file directly — the source path is considered easier and the correct approach when the old narration is baked into the file or the new narration has a different length. Confidence: 0.7
- When commissioning a new video with fresh assets, prefers reusing an existing video in the project as the structural template ("make another video like the lyson that is there", and again "make another animation video for radar like we did for others") — drops new images + narration audio into a folder, expects the assistant to discover them and mirror the reference scene's components/pattern. Confidence: 0.9
- Prefers using the narration audio directly as the master track without transcribing it — questioned why transcription was needed at all ("why do you need to transcribe. you could use the audio directly, right?") when scene cuts can be spread across the audio; transcription is seen as unnecessary overhead unless word-level timestamps are truly required. Confidence: 0.9

Asked about the 3D tooling ("is it 3js that is use for 3d animation?") and then directly commissioned a 3D demo in the stack ("ok, make a 3D airplane model flying") — treats the project as a playground for proving animation techniques, not just producing final videos.
